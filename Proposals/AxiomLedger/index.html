Where You Are

AxiomLedger has a differentiated product. The technology provides independent, continuous proof that digital entitlements match the real underlying assets — and records that cannot be altered. Goldlagerhaus proves it works in production.

Two years in, the challenge is not the product. It is finding the audience. The message has not been tested at scale, and the story has not yet been told in a way that makes the right companies come to you.

Our initial conversation focused on LinkedIn. That is the right channel. But before posting, it is worth spending a few weeks on foundational work — getting the story straight, mapping who it is for, and building a plan that every piece of content will serve. Done in that order, the content itself becomes faster to write, sharper in effect, and easier to evaluate.

This proposal is for that programme: a founder content engine that builds AxiomLedger's story from the ground up, and uses content creation to test and refine it in public.

Approach

Four workstreams, in the order they need to be built:

01

Messaging Framework

Before content, you need a clear and tested answer to four questions: what is AxiomLedger, who is it for, what problems does it solve, and what does it prove? This is the backbone for everything that follows. Without it, content is a series of disconnected posts. With it, every piece of content compounds on the last.

Core story

What AxiomLedger does, stated plainly and precisely — in a way that makes the right person immediately understand why it matters.

Audience mapping

Who the message is for: the roles, the institutions, the moments in the decision process where AxiomLedger becomes relevant.

Use cases

The specific scenarios where the technology solves a real problem — the cases that are most commercially urgent and most defensible to tell.

Measurable value

What AxiomLedger enables that a buyer can point to — in terms of risk, cost, compliance, or auditability — and why existing alternatives do not deliver the same thing.

02

Content Plan

Once the messaging framework is in place, I build a posting plan mapped to AxiomLedger's target sectors, partners, and commercial priorities. This is not a generic editorial calendar — it is a strategic document that maps every planned piece of content to a specific audience, a specific moment in the buying journey, and a specific outcome. Every post pulls in a commercial direction. The plan becomes the brief for everything that follows, so effort does not get spread thin across topics that do not serve the goal.

03

Article Sequence

Founder-bylined long-form articles are the highest-value content format for a B2B fintech audience. They establish authority, they circulate through institutional networks, and they give LinkedIn posts something of substance to point to. I write these from short interviews with you — in your voice, on your ideas — and deliver each piece with a short promotion guide so you know how to use it.

Articles are the asset. LinkedIn posts are the distribution. The cadence in Month 1 is heavier on the writing side to establish the corpus; from Month 2, posts and articles run together in a steady rhythm.

04

Website

Once the messaging has been tested in public — through articles, posts, and conversations — it is ready to be baked into the website. The site should be a credible backstop: the place you send someone after a conversation that wants to know more. I keep this as a separate project, quoted separately once the message is proven, so you are not investing in web infrastructure before you know what to say.

Structure

One ongoing engagement, roughly one day per week, heavier in Month 1.

Month 1 — Foundation

Front-loaded

The first month is heavier because it does the work that everything else depends on. Messaging framework, content plan, and first articles — all delivered before Month 2 begins so the ongoing cadence can run without friction.

Deliverables

  • • Messaging framework — core story, audience map, use cases, measurable value
  • • Content plan — posting plan mapped to target sectors and commercial priorities
  • • First two articles — written in your voice, from interview, with promotion guidance
  • • LinkedIn post series — initial batch mapped to the content plan

Month 2 Onward — Cadence

Ongoing
~1 day / week

Steady rhythm against the content plan — one to two articles per month, ongoing LinkedIn posts, and light iteration on the messaging as you learn what resonates. The content plan is a living document; we refine what is working and redirect what is not.

Deliverables

  • • One to two articles per month — founder-bylined, from interview, in your voice
  • • Ongoing LinkedIn post series — tied to each article and standalone topics
  • • Light messaging iteration — adjusting emphasis as you learn what lands

Website — separate project

Once the message has been tested in public and we know what is working, I will quote for a new site version that integrates it. Keeping this separate means you are not spending on web infrastructure before the story is ready.

What I'd Need From You

The lightest possible ask. The whole point of this model is that the burden on you is minimal.

Interview time up front

Two to three focused sessions in Month 1 to build the messaging framework and first articles. This is the heaviest lift; after it, the ongoing requirement drops significantly.

A short weekly conversation

Twenty to thirty minutes, ongoing. Enough to keep the content grounded in what is actually happening at AxiomLedger and to iterate on what is working.

Quick sign-off on drafts

I aim to get drafts right first time. I ask for one round of feedback only — no long revision cycles. The goal is to make sign-off fast, not to produce content that needs significant rework.
