Summary

Funding Circle has ten years of domain authority, proprietary lending data, and a product suite that applies to almost every financial moment a UK SME will face. Currently, SEO sits between three teams, with agency support. What it needs to scale is strategic ownership over how that authority is deployed across organic search.

Content is currently produced article-by-article against a narrow topic set, with limited structural support, no unified topic architecture, and a compliance process that was built to review campaigns or one-off articles rather than to sustain a publishing engine. The result is an output that is steady but reactive — and a channel that is underperforming its potential, especially given its proven ROI.

This proposal is for me to take ownership of that strategic layer on a fractional basis — two days a week for six months — and build the plan, architecture, publishing process, and writer operation needed to turn Funding Circle's organic channel into a scalable acquisition engine.

The Problem

Based on our conversations and a review of the last twelve months of Funding Circle's output, four structural issues are holding the channel back:

1

No unified topic strategy

Content is published reactively around calendar events (Budget, tax year, rate cuts) and product launches (FlexiPay, credit cards). There is no mapped universe of topics Funding Circle is actively trying to own, and no prioritisation framework for what should be built next.

2

A flat site architecture

Ten years of content sits in a chronological archive with limited internal structure. Search engines are not receiving clear topical signals, existing authority is not being concentrated into defined territories, and new content does not compound on old.

3

A compliance process that cannot scale

A two-person compliance team processes every article through the same pipeline regardless of topic or risk profile. Pushing ten articles a month is already difficult.

4

Execution without a strategic frame

Blue Array is producing ten to fifteen articles a month against briefs that are not anchored to a clear topic strategy. Without that frame, even good execution cannot compound — each article is a standalone asset rather than a contribution to a broader authority build.

The Gap

Funding Circle has strong people across brand, CRO, and SEO — but none of them are positioned to do this work. Paul's focus is CRO, testing, and site optimisation. Jo's focus is brand stewardship and campaign-led creative. James is running the SEO channel while managing paid performance and other priority channels.

What is missing is someone whose single job is to align these functions around a shared content strategy, bring external expertise on how to build and run a content machine at scale in a regulated environment, and hold the whole picture — plan, architecture, compliance, execution — in one head.

Approach

Writing more articles against an unclear plan, or pushing more volume through a compliance process that is already struggling, will yield results. I've focused on four elements, in the order they need to be solved:

01

Content Plan and Keyword Alignment

The starting point is a clear view of what Funding Circle should be trying to own in organic search, and why. This would be a map of topic territories — the full set of subjects a UK SME owner searches for across their commercial and financial life — prioritised by commercial relevance, competitive vulnerability, and Funding Circle's ability to credibly win them.

Topic Territory Current Position Opportunity
Core lending products Held: product pages and reactive posts Deepen with scenario-led and sector-specific content
Tax, regulation and policy Strong reactive coverage (Budget, rate cuts, tax year) Move from reactive to evergreen authority
Cash flow, working capital, financial management Light coverage: handful of articles per year Major gap. High commercial intent, low current output
Alternative finance Minimal: a few product-led pieces Entire vertical largely uncontested by Funding Circle
Sector-specific finance (construction, retail, logistics) Absent Proprietary lending data supports deep vertical plays
Operational content (hiring, software, growth) Absent Competitors winning here. Out of scope for Phase 1, worth flagging

Each territory then gets broken down into keyword clusters, each cluster anchored to a commercial goal, and each cluster sized against realistic publishing cadence. This becomes the brief for everything that follows: writer briefs, compliance workflow design, reporting, and the architectural decisions below.

02

Site Architecture

Once the topic plan is clear, the next question is how Funding Circle's content should be structured to actually own those territories. The current chronological blog is not built for scale. It publishes content into a limited archive with some internal linking, no visible topical hierarchy, and no way to concentrate authority signals around specific verticals.

Three architectural moves I would evaluate in Phase 1, in order of ambition:

A

Hub and spoke within the main resource centre

The fastest, lowest-risk move. Reorganise the existing archive into defined topic hubs with pillar pages and deep internal linking. Immediate structural benefit without touching the underlying domain.

B

Dedicated topic verticals on the main domain

Build out /resources/business-loans/, /resources/cash-flow/, /resources/growth/ as fully structured sub-sections with their own navigation, landing pages, and internal architecture.

C

Topic subdomains for priority verticals

The most aggressive structural move. A dedicated subdomain (e.g. assetfinance.fundingcircle.com) for a single high-priority vertical gives search engines an unambiguous topical signal and allows that vertical to compound authority independently of the main blog. This is the move that makes a broader keyword strategy actually work at scale, because it provides the clearest possible topical signal to search. It is also the highest-investment option and would only make sense for a territory where Funding Circle has committed to dominance.

(This can be discussed – while it's an important one for future growth, it does add short term work to set up subdomains. That said, it's easier to start with this structure in place than retrofit later, I can say from experience).

03

Compliance

The compliance bottleneck determines how fast this machine can move. Current process applies the same review intensity to every article regardless of topic or risk, but this could be streamlined.

Risk-tiered review paths

Tax-specific content goes through a tax review path. Product claims go through a product-and-legal path. General educational and evergreen content follows a lightweight scan path.

Pre-flagged danger zones in drafts

Writers flag regulated terminology, specific numerical claims, and product comparisons at draft stage. Reviewers scan for highlighted sections first, reducing line-by-line reading.

Brief templates that front-load compliance

Known regulatory and tonal constraints are built into the brief itself, so writers avoid problems rather than generating content that later needs unwinding.

Pre-approved vocabulary and claims bank

For recurring topics (rates, products, eligibility), pre-approved language patterns reduce the review burden to near zero on repeat assets.

I would build this in direct collaboration with the compliance team — to support them, not create more work for them.

04

Production

Once the plan, architecture and compliance process are in place does it make sense to set writer capacity. Once we have a full list of what we want to cover, we decide how fast we want to get there, which then determines necessary resources.

1

I write the first pilots myself

Four to six articles in Phase 1 to set tone, test the compliance workflow, and produce assets that prove the approach before any external writers are onboarded.

2

I bring in vetted writers I have worked with before

Their briefs come from me, their drafts come back to me for review and revision, and only then go into the compliance workflow.

3

Blue Array's scope gets redefined, not removed

Their strongest work is keyword and competitor research. I would recommend repositioning their scope toward deeper keyword research, which I can then turn into brand-aligned briefs. This keeps their value in the machine while removing the weakest part of the current arrangement.

4

Focus content on depth and authority

With deeper research and content-benchmarked writers, we can create brand-aligned, data-led, genuinely helpful content that ranks.

5

Volume emerges from the system

The first target should be consistency at a pace compliance can absorb, against a plan everyone has signed off. Volume then scales as the operational constraints are lifted — measured monthly, reviewed quarterly.
